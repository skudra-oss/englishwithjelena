Question 2 · language

How to build a Question 2 paragraph

Each paragraph follows four steps. Repeat them for every example you choose from the text.

1 · PointName a piece of language that creates an effect.
“The writer uses [language feature]…”
2 · ExampleGive a short quotation: a word or phrase from the text.
3 · ExplainExplain how this language creates a feeling, an atmosphere, or shows a character’s emotion.
4 · LinkUse different phrases and linking words so you don’t repeat yourself.

Template: describing atmosphere

The writer creates a adjective atmosphere using language feature. For example, “quote”. This conveys effect on the reader / character’s emotion, suggesting interpretation. The choice of word / phrase also makes the reader feel feeling.
Tip: always tie the feeling to a specific word or phrase in the text, not just a general description.

Instead of “This suggests…”

Use different phrases so your writing doesn’t get repetitive.

  • This shows that…Это показывает, что…
  • This implies…Это подразумевает…
  • This conveys…Это передаёт…
  • This creates a sense of…Это создаёт ощущение…
  • This gives the impression that…Это создаёт впечатление, что…
  • This highlights…Это подчёркивает…
  • This reflects…Это отражает…
  • This portrays…Это изображает…
  • This evokes… (a feeling, a sense of…)Это вызывает…
  • This demonstrates…Это демонстрирует (показывает)…

Other useful linking phrases

  • The word / phrase “[quote]”…
  • The writer’s use of [adjective] language…
  • Through [word / phrase], the writer…хочет сказать / показать… (что)
  • The effect of this is…

Types of atmosphere and feelings

The setting

Setting means where and when a story takes place. Writers use it to add effect and build atmosphere.

Settings have connotations (ассоциации): ideas and feelings we connect with them. The same place can feel completely different at another time: think of a school after dark, then the same school full of pupils at lunchtime.

Night, dark, isolated

Used in ghost stories and scary stories → scary, eerie, mysterious

A sunny day

→ serene, uplifting, happy

Gloomy, rainy, windy weather

→ anxious, tense, scary, suspenseful; often reflects the character’s feelings

Nature

→ calm, serene; being at peace, “at one with nature”

A historical period

e.g. World War Two → eerie, tense, serious, heroic, patriotic, moving

Difficult words

Atmosphere

An atmosphere of wealth, a luxury lifestyle and indulgence (потакание своим желаниям, излишество).

to indulge (баловать себя) = to let yourself enjoy something pleasant, often too much

Who tells the story?

The narrator (рассказчик) is the person telling the story. This is not always the writer.

1st person: “I…” · 3rd person / observer: “he…”

“I watched his guests”: a 1st-person narrator who watches from the edge. He describes the parties but isn’t part of them.

Discuss

Find all the words that show movement (движение). Why does the writer fill the scene with movement? How does the narrator seem to feel about the parties?

Question

How does the writer use language to create an atmosphere of wealth and indulgence?

The writer creates a lively, luxurious atmosphere using a simile. For example, men and girls “came and went like moths”. This conveys how Gatsby’s guests are pulled towards his glittering parties, the way moths fly to a light, suggesting they come for the glamour rather than for Gatsby himself. The writer’s use of repetition in “his blue gardens” and “his beach” highlights how much Gatsby owns, which gives the impression of enormous wealth. Through the metaphor “his Rolls-Royce became an omnibus”, the writer shows that even a luxury car is used like a public bus, which evokes a sense of indulgence and parties that never end.
